# Pharmacy Pitch — Pocket Card

**Use:** Print or keep on phone. Spoken lines only. Full playbook lives in `PHARMACY_WALK_IN_PITCH.md`.

---

## 10-Second Line

> I help pharmacies get online, keep refill customers, and use AI to cut busywork — without replacing the pharmacist.

---

## 60-Second Line

> I help pharmacies keep customers in a world where chains win on convenience.
>
> Practically: a digital front door customers can use, CRM so refill patients get remembered, less phone chaos for your team, and AI as a junior assistant for repetitive questions — never as a fake pharmacist.
>
> Customers get easier access. You keep more recurring business. Staff get time back.
>
> If that sounds relevant, give me 15 quiet minutes and I’ll show you the simplest version for a pharmacy like yours.

## Stage Lines

### 0 — Entry
> Hi — is the owner or manager free for two minutes?
> I’m [Name]. I work with pharmacies on getting online, customer follow-up, and simplifying day-to-day operations.
> If now is bad, tell me a quieter time and I’ll come back.

**Busy:**
> Totally fair. When is least crazy — mid-morning or after 4? Five minutes then is enough.

### 1 — Open
> I won’t waste your time. I’m not here to dump software on you.
> I want to understand how you handle three things: people finding you, customers coming back for refills, and the admin load on the team.

### 2 — Best questions (pick 3–5)
1. If someone new nearby searches for a pharmacy, what do they see for you?
2. After a first visit, how do you stay in touch — or do people just disappear?
3. Do refill customers mostly remember on their own, or do many forget and fill elsewhere?
4. What eats the most staff time that is *not* counseling — phone, “is it ready?”, stock questions?
5. If you could remove one repetitive task from the counter tomorrow, what would it be?
6. Have you tried a website or CRM before? What stalled?
7. If something could show value in 30 days without disrupting the counter, would a short look be worth it?

### 3 — Reframe
> You’re already good at pharmacy care. That’s not the gap.
> The gap is usually visibility, follow-up, and operations.
> Chains often win less on care and more on convenience + memory.
> So the job isn’t “buy technology.” It’s: make it easy to choose you, hard to forget you, and lighter for your staff to run.

### 4 — Four pillars (bridge)
> Customers get easier access and clearer communication.
> Retention comes from remembering them at the right moment.
> The business keeps more revenue and wastes less staff time.
> AI is the assistant that makes those three affordable to run every day — not a replacement for your expertise.

#### Pillar bites (say only what matches their pain)

**Customer / website**
> A useful website is a 24/7 receptionist — hours, refill requests, fewer wasted trips, caregivers can help.

**Retention / CRM**
> You don’t lose customers in an argument. You lose them in silence.
> CRM means: capture once, know when they may need you, remind them helpfully, keep the relationship warm.

**Business**
> More of your hard-won customers stay. Your team wastes less time. You own the relationship instead of hoping foot traffic is enough.

**AI**
> AI is a junior staff member that never sleeps — common questions, intake, reminders.
> If it’s clinical or uncertain, it escalates. You stay the professional.

### 5 — Proof
> Can I show you 60 seconds of what this feels like for a customer — not a slide, the actual flow?

**No demo:**
> Customer needs a refill at 8pm → submits in 30 seconds → morning list is clean → reminder before the next cycle. Simple. Human when it matters.

### 6 — Close
**Soft:**
> Would it help if I came back for 15 minutes when it’s quieter and mapped this against your customers and phone load?

**Choice:**
> Tomorrow mid-morning, or a 15-minute call after closing — which is easier?

**Audit:**
> I can do a free look at how you appear online and where refill customers might be leaking. If I find nothing useful, you still get clarity. Fair?

**Stall:**
> One question so I don’t come back with the wrong plan: bigger priority — getting found, stopping refill drop-off, or reducing phone/admin load?

## Objections (short)

| They say | You say |
|----------|---------|
| We have a website | Good — most exist but don’t work. I’d check if it creates requests and retained customers. |
| We’re too small | Small is when this helps. Living list + reminders + fewer repeated questions — not enterprise software. |
| Customers are older | Many still walk in. Caregivers and phones still matter. Simple “ready for pickup” texts reduce frustration. |
| No budget | Start with audit or tight pilot. Hidden cost is silent refill loss and phone hours. |
| Tech failed before | Common. Version one stays boring: one job for web, one for CRM, one for AI — until it’s habit. |
| Ask my partner | I’ll leave a one-pager. Happy to do 10 minutes with both of you. |
| Is AI safe? | Assist and escalate. Clinical stays with licensed professionals. Rules and consent are part of setup. |
| Just leave a brochure | Appreciate your time. If refill drop-off or phone load becomes a headache, I’m easy to reach. |
